## Customer problem

Validate the proposed job: “When my usual meal does not appeal today, help me choose something manageable and remember what worked.” This is a hypothesis, not an established churn cause.

## Research plan

- Interview six recently lapsed users who switched tools, six retained users, and six new or struggling users.
- Include medication users and people not using medication, varied food preferences, and different levels of tracking experience.
- Observe eight sessions covering meal entry, correction, goal setting, and planning tomorrow.
- Ask switchers to reconstruct the event immediately before leaving and demonstrate their current workaround.
- Do not prompt participants to endorse an AI coach.

## Instrumentation

Track first log, failed save, correction, repeat meal, plan creation, plan use, recommendation exposure, recommendation acceptance, return visits, and optional cancellation reason. Exclude meal text, symptoms, medication details, email, and photos from general analytics.

## Decision criteria

- Proceed with Appetite Compass if at least 6 of 10 recruited target-segment concept testers independently describe the job as frequent and at least 7 can select and save a suitable meal without help.
- Treat these as qualitative gates, not statistical proof.
- If faster logging dominates, move assisted capture ahead of recommendation work.
- If trust or relevance fails, retain reviewed human-curated suggestions and revise the concept.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Baseline activation and W4 useful-retention funnel is documented
- [ ] Research sample and interview guide are approved
- [ ] 18 interviews and 8 observed task sessions are synthesized
- [ ] Findings distinguish observations, interpretations, and hypotheses
- [ ] Week 2 proceed, revise, or stop decision is recorded
